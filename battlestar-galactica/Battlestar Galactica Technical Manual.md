# BATTLESTAR GALACTICA: TECHNICAL MANUAL

**COLONIAL FLEET SURVIVOR FLEET REFERENCE, FIRST EDITION**

*Seventh entry in the collected set. Prepared in the tradition of the Star Trek Writers'/DS9 Technical Manuals: an in-universe reference consolidating established canon, with clearly marked engineering extrapolation.*

## ABOUT THIS DOCUMENT

This manual documents the technology of the Colonial Fleet and its enemy as observed during the exodus following the Fall of the Twelve Colonies. It supplements character work, this setting's drama is survival without becoming what hunted you, and every system here is rationed accordingly.

**Edition pin.** This First Edition reflects the fleet's status quo as of **Fleet Year One, approximately 47,000 survivors** (see §1.5).

**Conventions used throughout:**

## PART ONE: THE THEATER

### 1.0 Introduction

One battlestar survived the annihilation of twelve worlds because she was about to become a museum. Everything in this manual, every Viper, every ration point, every election, exists inside that sentence.

### 1.1 Strategic Situation

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Fleet | ~80 surviving ships escorting Galactica **[E]**; population ~47,000 |
| Destination | Earth (mythical location; coordinates unknown) |
| Enemy | Cylon forces — raiders, basestars, humanoid agents embedded in the fleet |
| Doctrine | Run dark, jump often, trust no network |

Defining facts:

1. **Networked computers lost the war.** Cylons infiltrate wired systems; Colonial doctrine now runs on paper, analog telephones, and radio discipline.
2. **Resources are the real clock.** Water, tylium fuel, ammunition, and people are all finite; the fleet's true course is plotted against supply, not stars.
3. **The enemy looks like us now.** Humanoid Cylons pass every test but loyalty over time. Screening is procedural, psychological, and imperfect.

### 1.2 Consolidated Timeline

| Era | Event |
|---|---|
| ~4,000 years prior | Exile on Kobol; the Thirteen Tribes depart; Earth becomes scripture |
| ~52 years prior | First Cylon War: created slaves rebel; armistice; Cylons withdraw beyond the Armistice Line |
| 40 years of silence | No contact. Colonies demilitarize; networks proliferate anyway |
| Fall of the Colonies | Coordinated nuclear strike via backdoored CNP defense software; Galactica (un-networked, decommissioning) survives to lead exodus |
| Fleet Year One | New Caprica settlement and occupation; rescue; the search for Earth continues. **Pin** |

### 1.3 Assessment of Forces

**Colonial Fleet (remnant).** One battlestar, one second-hand Pegasus later in the record **[W]**, dwindling Viper wings, and a civilian fleet held together by martial law and hope.

**The Cylons.** See Part Six. Resurrection-equipped, monotheist, patient, and fractured by their own factions' disagreement about humanity.
### 1.4 Founding Technologies

**In brief:** Colonial civilization rests on three mature systems: solid-state artificial gravity (no spin required), tylium-fueled sublight and jump drives, and, post-Fall, a deliberate *retreat* from networked computing. The Cylon War was fought by analog descendants of a digital empire, which is both irony and survival strategy.

### 1.5 Status Quo Snapshot (Fleet Year One)

| Domain | Status |
|---|---|
| Battlestar | Galactica (BS-75) — de-militarized hull reactivated; flight pods intact, Viper strength rebuilding from museum stock |
| Civilian fleet | ~80 ships under escort; martial law after sabotage waves |
| Government | President (civilian line of succession exercised); Quorum suspended/restored as politics demand |
| Cylon contact | Periodic raider probes; humanoid agents suspected inside fleet **[W]** |
| Course | Earth — bearing set by scripture and hope in equal measure |

### 2.1a Deck Zones

| Deck | Function |
|---|---|

| Marine/brig levels | Detention, armory, tribunal room |

### 2.1b Flight Operations Doctrine

- **Launch tempo:** Vipers off the tubes in seconds, the tube launch is Galactica's signature heartbeat.
- **Recovery under fire:** flak curtain opens a corridor; pilots land blind through artillery.
- **Raptor operations:** stealth recon, EW jamming, rescue, nuke delivery, the fleet's true multirole backbone.

### 2.2 Defensive Doctrine

- **DRADIS:** detection/tracking suite (radio-optical hybrid **[E]**).
- **Flak umbrella:** anti-air artillery fired as a curtain for Viper recovery, Galactica's most cinematic defense.
- **FTL jumps:** tactical teleport via jump drives; range per jump limited, coordinates computed offline. Fleet jumps are choreographed mass events.

### 2.3 Life Support & Rations

Fleet food runs from agricultural ships; water from recycling ice hauls (the ice crisis nearly ended the fleet in month one). Galactica's own stores feed command crew; everyone else eats what the fleet grows.

## PART THREE: FTL OPERATIONS

**In brief:** Colonial jump drives fold space point-to-point: instantaneous translation, no transit medium, no navigation sensors at the far end until arrival. Accuracy is a function of computation time, blind jumps scatter ships across light-years.

- **Fleet jumps:** every ship must jump within a timed window or be left; stragglers are dead by definition.
- **Tylium economy:** jumps and sublight burn consume refined tylium; the fleet's fuel state is its true strategic map.
- **Cylon superiority:** Cylon basestars jump farther and faster; Colonial survival math is built on running.

### 3.x Jump Operations Procedure

**Fleet jump sequence (standard):**

1. Navigation computes coordinates offline, paper math, checked twice (errors scatter ships into deep space).
2. FTL drives spool; fleet receives the order and window.
3. Ships translate on the mark, stragglers are abandoned by doctrine, mourned by policy.
4. Post-jump: DRADIS sweep, headcount, fuel audit before the next breath.

**Range/computation trade:** longer jumps need exponentially more computation; combat jumps are short and rough. Blind jumps are gambles with everyone's coordinates. Cylon edge: basestars compute faster and range farther, Colonial survival is built on running better, not fighting better.

### 3.x DRADIS Performance **[E]**

Detection ranges are classified Colonial Navy figures; observed behavior: raiders resolve at tens of kilometers, basestars at hundreds, stealth profiles (Raptor-dark) can close unflagged. Raptor jammers degrade but never blind the net.

### 5.2 The Network Ban

**In brief:** CNP defense software backdoor caused the Fall; post-Fall doctrine bans networked computers fleet-wide. Radios, paper plots, and hand-carved flight sticks are counterintelligence now. Every automation request is denied for reasons everyone understands and resents anyway.

### 5.3 Strategic Materials

**Tylium fuel** (mined/refined from tylium ore; single refinery loss nearly ended the fleet) · **water** (recycling economics) · **people** (pilots especially, training pipeline longer than the war's patience).

## PART SIX: THREAT DOSSIERS

### 6.0 The Cylons

**In brief:** Created servants who rebelled, left, returned stronger, and committed genocide, then complicated everything by looking like us.

- **Centurions:** chrome soldiers; armored, loyal, pre-war models still honor the armistice's shape.
- **Raiders:** living fighters (§4.2).
- **Humanoid model lines (twelve total):** indistinguishable colonists; resurrection transfers consciousness on death; some defect in practice if not in programming.

| Known lines (by role) | Observed function |
|---|---|
| Diplomat/infiltrator types | Fleet penetration, negotiation-as-weapon |
| Soldier types | Ground combat, execution squads |
| Hybrid-adjacent handlers | Basestar interface management |
| Final Five | Hidden elder models embedded in Colonial history itself — revealed progressively **[W]** |

- **Hybrids:** organic interfaces piloting basestars through prophecy-streamed command language, half oracle, half operating system. Their "nonsense" predicts events with uncomfortable accuracy; interpreting hybrid-speak is a Cylon religious discipline and a Colonial intelligence skill.
- **The Final Five:** hidden variable, older models embedded in Colonial history itself **[W]**.

**Engagement rules:** Never trust a clean network. Never assume a raider stays dead. Always ask which model you're talking to *after* the third meeting, not the first.

## PART EIGHT: A FLEET BESTIARY

### 8.0 Resurrection Technology

**What:** Cylon orbital stations downloading consciousness of any proximate model death into new bodies.
**Where:** Resurrection ships/space; hub-dependent **[W]**.
**Cost of contact:** Death loses its teaching power. A species that cannot die cannot learn caution, or mercy.

### 8.2 Kobol & the Tomb of Athena

**What:** Birthworld of humanity's gods-era; navigation archive disguised as tomb.
**Where:** Beyond the Armistice Line, mythically mapped.
**Cost of contact:** Scripture verified. Faith-based navigation works exactly once per question.

### 8.3 The Eye of Jupiter

**What:** Star in nova transition sacred to both peoples; temple on its algonith-class moon **[E]**.
**Cost of contact:** Two armies, one miracle, zero shots fired, the exception that defines the rule.

### 8.4 New Caprica Occupation Pattern

**What:** Case study: settlement without escape capacity invites occupation.
**Where:** Any habitable world short of defenses.
**Cost of contact:** Measured in collaboration files and resistance graves.
