# BABYLON 5: TECHNICAL MANUAL

**BABYLON STATION OPERATIONS REFERENCE, FIRST EDITION**

*Fourth entry in the collected set, companion to the Stargate program volumes. Prepared in the tradition of the Star Trek Writers'/DS9 Technical Manuals: an in-universe reference consolidating broadcast canon, with clearly marked engineering extrapolation.*

---

## ABOUT THIS DOCUMENT

This manual provides the detailed technical background that stories aboard Babylon 5 and across the known galaxy sometimes need. It supplements character work, never replaces it, Babylon 5's stories are about choices made under pressure of history, and every technical system here exists to raise the price of those choices.

**Edition pin.** This First Edition reflects the station's status quo as of **2260 CE, the Shadow War open, Babylon 5 in open secession from the Earth Alliance** (see §1.5). Events after that point are not reflected here.

**Conventions used throughout:**

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *(no marker)* | Established by on-screen canon as broadcast (the TV movies count as broadcast) |
| **[E]** | Extrapolation — internally consistent engineering detail where the screen was silent |
| **[W]** | Writer's latitude note — guidance on how hard this rule binds you |

Where this manual conflicts with a filmed episode, the episode wins. Log the conflict in Appendix C.

**Structure:** Part One sets the galactic theater. Part Two is the station herself. Part Three covers jumpgate and hyperspace operations. Part Four carries vessel specifications. Part Five covers weapons and equipment. Part Six is the threat dossier, read it before writing any Shadow, Vorlon, or Psi Corps scene. Part Seven defines command structure. Part Eight catalogs phenomena. Appendices carry glossary, conversions, the canon log, and index.

## PART ONE: THE GALACTIC THEATER

### 1.0 Introduction

Babylon 5 is the last of the Babylon Project: five kilometers of diplomatic neutrality parked in orbit around a dead world at the galaxy's busiest jumpgate junction, built so that a war ten centuries old could end at a table instead of in orbit. Her crew are humans running a hotel for empires, every one of whom has fought everyone else within living memory.

**In brief:** Where other installations defend territory, Babylon 5 manufactures something rarer: conversations between powers that have no other common ground.

### 1.1 The Strategic Situation

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Theater | Known Galaxy — Earth Alliance space, the Rim, and the League of Non-Aligned Worlds |
| Installation | Babylon 5 Station, Epsilon III orbit |
| Complement | ~250,000 residents and transients **[E]**; staff complement ~2,500 **[E]** |
| Posture | Independent state as of the edition pin — seceded from the Earth Alliance under President Clark's dictatorship |
| Neutrality | Charter-bound open port: four governments plus the League maintain permanent delegations |

Three facts define every decision aboard:

1. **Neutrality is the product.** The station survives because everyone needs a place to talk. Every arrest, every favor, every intercepted shipment spends from that account.
2. **Two ancient wars run underneath everything.** The Shadows and the Vorlons have recruited younger races as proxies for a million years; the current war is their argument made kinetic.
3. **Earth cannot help.** The homeworld is under a totalitarian presidency; the station's garrison is loyal to the charter, not the flag, a fact with a body count.

**[W]** B5 stories are history stories: individuals bent against structures larger than they are. Technology should always serve that pressure, never solve it cheaply.

### 1.2 Consolidated Timeline

| Era | Event |
|---|---|
| ~1,000,000 years ago | First Ones dominate; Vorlons and Shadows become rival shepherds of younger races — order versus chaos as teaching doctrine |
| ~10,000 years ago | Most First Ones depart beyond the Rim; the two shepherds remain |
| ~1,260 years ago | Last great Shadow War. Valen leads the united Minbari to victory; the Grey Council founds on that victory; Shadows sleep |
| 2229–2232 | Dilgar War: the Dilgar rampage through the League until the Earth Alliance enters and breaks them |
| 2245–2248 | Earth–Minbari War: first contact catastrophe at the hands of an Earth ship; Minbari genocide campaign halted at the Line when they discover Valen's soul among humans. Earth broken, humbled, paranoid |
| 2248–2257 | Babylon Stations 1–4: sabotage, bankruptcy, disappearance (Babylon 4 — see §8.2). Babylon 5 completes under private Earth financing |
| 2258 | Station's first year: Kosh's arrival, assassination politics, Raider suppression, the Soul Hunter incident |
| 2259 | Death of President Santiago; Vice-President Clark assumes power. Shadow vessel recovered at Zagros; first open Shadow move in a thousand years |
| 2260 | Shadow War erupts openly. Babylon 5 secedes rather than submit to Clark. **Edition pin** |
### 1.3 Technological Assessment of Major Powers

*(Full dossiers in Part Six. Format follows the set's assessment convention.)*

**Earth Alliance.** The youngest major power, barely a century of jumpspace travel, compensating with mass production and stubbornness. Omega-class destroyers rotate for gravity; Starfury wings punch above their weight; Earthforce doctrine is artillery-first and unimaginative. Politically the Alliance is the theater's sick man: a presidency sliding into fascism while its best fleet sits at Babylon 5.

**Minbari Federation.** Technically superior to everyone (gravitic drives, stealth hulls, beam cannons measured in seconds-to-kill), constrained by caste politics and religious law from using any of it aggressively since Valen's war. Their fleet is the galaxy's trump card, held by a warrior caste spoiling for redemption.

**Centauri Republic.** Declining imperial power running on prestige, slave-adjacent client states, and superb light ships. Their renaissance under Cartagia's court is the Shadow alliance made flesh, nostalgia weaponized.

**Narn Regime.** Occupied for a century by the Centauri, liberated by guerrilla attrition; now rebuilding on salvaged Centauri tech and bitter clarity. Their heavy cruisers are honest, slow, and expendable, like their strategy after the fall of their homeworld.

**The League of Non-Aligned Worlds.** A dozen minor powers (Drazi, Brakiri, Abbai, Pak'ma'ra, Vree and kin) whose combined fleets matter and whose separate fleets do not. Babylon 5 exists to make them add up.

**The Shadows.** See §6.0. The oldest power in play: ships that think, allies that vanish, and a philosophy that breeds strength through engineered catastrophe.

**The Vorlons.** See §6.1. Order as an ideology, inscrutable, patient, and generous in ways that always invoice later.

### 1.4 Founding Technologies of Note

**In brief:** Three technologies shape every strategic equation in this volume:

1. **Jumpgates and hyperspace beacons**, the only FTL transit system (§3.0); controlling gates means controlling trade, war, and escape.
2. **Artificial gravity asymmetry**, Minbari/Vorlon gravitic mastery versus Earth's rotating-section workaround defines ship design philosophy galaxy-wide.
3. **Telepathy**, a human minority (one in a thousand) with commercial, military, and political value enough to justify an institution (the Psi Corps) that functions as a state within the state.

### 1.5 Status Quo Snapshot (Edition Pin: 2260 CE)

| Domain | Status |
|---|---|
| Station | Independent; secession defended by Starfury wing + White Star support **[W]** |
| Earth relations | Open hostility — Clark's regime lists B5 command as traitors; Nightwatch infiltrators purged or hiding aboard |
| Shadow War | Open. Colony worlds vanishing; League members requesting B5's coalition leadership |
| Minbari | Religious caste backs the station's war effort via covert White Star operations; warrior caste obstructs — Federation near civil split |
| Centauri | Shadow-aligned at court level; Republic forces hitting Narn and League targets |
| Vorlons | One ambassador resident (Kosh); minimal, cryptic, decisive when it moves |
| Psi Corps | Officially Earth institution; unofficially playing every side |

## PART TWO: BABYLON 5 STATION

### 2.0 Station Overview

**In brief:** The last of the Babylon Project's diplomatic stations: a five-mile O'Neill-cylinder habitat spun for gravity, strung along a spine of docking bays, wrapped in the known galaxy's densest diplomatic charter. Built by Earth, financed by Minbari gold, staffed by everyone.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Class | Babylon-class diplomatic station (final unit of five) |
| Length | 8,064.5 m (stated on-screen in "And Now for a Word") |
| Displacement | ~2.5 million metric tons **[E]** |
| Gravity | Rotational — ~0.9 g in habitation ring **[E]**; zero-g spine and docking bays |
| Complement | ~2,500 staff **[E]**; 250,000 residents/transients capacity **[E]** |
| Autonomy | Self-sufficient for years: agriculture ring, fusion plant, ore processing |
| Defense | 48 SA-23E Starfuries (two squadrons) + interceptor grid + light pulse cannon array **[E]** |

**Design language:** functional Earthforce industrial, grey-green cylinder, blue-lit command sphere at the forward end, red-and-white docking spines. She is not beautiful; she is *working*.

### 2.01 Zone Deep-Dive

**Blue Sector (command spine).**

| Facility | Function |
|---|---|
| Command & Control | Traffic, defense, jumpgate ops (§2.2) |
| Medlab 1–3 | Primary care, quarantine isolation, xenobiology labs |
| Station jail / holding | Charter-processing detention |
| Officer quarters & war-room annexes | Briefing theater adjacent to C&C |
| Ambassadorial airlocks | Delegation docking with diplomatic privilege screening |

**Green Sector (embassy ring).** Each major power holds a self-sealing wing with its own atmosphere mix on request, Vorlon quarters cannot be entered by any younger race's key. Green Sector is extraterritorial ground by charter; security incidents there are treaty events, not crimes.

**Grey Sector & Downbelow.** Industry, maintenance shops, recycling plants, and the undocumented population living in maintenance crawls. Station security patrols the corridors; nobody patrols Downbelow except its own.

**The Zócalo.** Open market on the habitation ring: commerce, smuggling, news, and the closest thing the station has to a public square. Every plot that needs "somewhere neutral aboard a neutral station" happens here.

**Carousel agriculture ring.** Hydroponic parks doubling as food production and the station's only open green space, weddings, duels, and secret meetings all book the same gardens.

### 2.1 Structures and Zones

| Zone | Function | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Blue Sector | Command & Control, medlabs, officer quarters, jail | Staff only |
| Green Sector | Diplomatic quarters — one wing per embassy | Delegation-controlled |
| Grey Sector | Industry, maintenance, Downbelow's slums | Mixed — the station's conscience lives here |
| Cargo/Sector codes | Warehousing, processing | Commercial |
| Habitation ring (Carousel) | Agriculture parks, housing, Zócalo market | Public |
| Docking spines & bays | Customs, freight, fighter launch rails | Controlled |

**Downbelow** is not on any official chart: the low-ceilinged crawl between Grey and cargo sectors where the undocumented live. Every station story eventually goes there; it is where the charter's promises meet rent.

### 2.2 Command Systems

**Command & Control (C&C).** The blue sphere at the bow: traffic control, defense coordination, jumpgate operation, and the interceptor grid. Watchstanders manage thousands of daily movements; C&C is the single point where the station's neutrality is enforced minute-to-minute.

**Station operations doctrine:** the commanding officer holds civil authority inside the charter and military authority over the garrison, a deliberately doubled mandate that has forced every commander to choose which hat dies first when Earth and the charter conflict.

### 2.3 Computer Systems

Earthforce-standard networked mainframes with voice interface layers; no sapient AI aboard **[W]**, resist the urge; the show's intelligence threats are institutional, not silicon.

- Customs and security databases track every arrival's declared purpose; Downbelow exists in the gap between database and reality.
- The station's charter text outranks station regulations in law, a hierarchy tested on-screen repeatedly.
- Encryption: Earthforce standard plus delegation-specific ciphers; intercepts are C&C's quiet specialty.
### 2.4 Power Generation

**In brief:** Eight fusion reactors **[E]** along the zero-g spine feed the rotation drive, life support, defense grid, and the jumpgate's capacitors.

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| Fusion plant cluster | Primary; fuel from the agriculture ring's waste cycle plus ore-processing byproducts |
| Jumpgate capacitor bank | The gate draws station-scale surges per activation — scheduled against defense-grid readiness |
| Solar/auxiliary | Emergency margin only |

**Power discipline:** the interceptor grid and the jumpgate compete for peak draw. A gate activation during an attack is a command decision, not a switch, canon demonstrated when the grid had to choose between fighters and escape routes.

### 2.5 Defensive Systems

**The interceptor grid.** Webbed emitter panels across the hull that fire pulsed energy to detonate incoming ordnance at standoff range, Earthforce's signature answer to superior alien firepower. Effectiveness scales with charge; sustained bombardment strips it section by section.

**Pulse cannon array.** Station-scale pulse batteries for close defense; adequate against raiders, symbolic against capital ships.

**Starfury wing.** Forty-eight SA-23E interceptors (§4.1) launched from spine rails, the station's real sword. Launch tempo under combat: seconds per fighter down the rail **[E]**.

**Structural doctrine:** the station cannot outrun or outfight a battle fleet. Her defenses buy hours for diplomacy, evacuation, or the cavalry, never victory in open battle. Every siege story should honor that math.

### 2.6 Docking and Bay Operations

Six major bays handle freighters, liners, and delegation cutters; fighter rails run parallel. Customs is the station's first line of both revenue and security, every crate logged, every hold scanned, every lie filed for later leverage.

### 2.7 Sensors and Communications

- **Long-range:** hyperspace beacon monitoring doubles as strategic sensor watch, ship movements between gates are visible to anyone reading the beacons (§3.0).
- **Tachyon comm relays:** real-time transmission via tachyon channels within linked space; B5 relays diplomatic traffic for the League.
- **Minbari sensors outclass everything here** — a standing intelligence asymmetry in the Federation's favor.

### 2.8 Life Support and Environment

Closed-cycle atmosphere with sector-level scrubbing; agriculture ring supplies food and CO₂ processing. Downbelow's atmosphere is technically the same air and socially another world. Quarantine medicine is excellent, Medlab 1 handled the Drafa plague (§8.5), but bed capacity is finite; plague stories must respect triage.

### 2.9 Emergency Operations

| Condition | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Intrusion | Boarding action, sabotage | Security lockdown by zone; fighter alert |
| Bomb threat | Charter violation | Customs trace + Grey Sector sweep |
| Siege | Capital threat | Grid full-power, civilians to shelter, gate reserved for evacuation |
| Secession | Government illegitimate | Charter invocation — executed at pin date; garrison loyalty decided it |

## PART THREE: JUMPGATE AND HYPERSPACE OPERATIONS

### 3.0 The Jump Network

**In brief:** FTL travel in the known galaxy runs exclusively through hyperspace, a layered dimension entered via fixed jumpgates (vortex generators of Elder-race vintage) or ship-mounted jump engines. Inside, there are no stars: navigation is by beacon chain, and leaving the beacons is how crews vanish.

**Rules of the road:**

- **Gates are furniture of empires.** Who builds a gate owns who passes through it; the Shadows' war strategy begins with destroying gates and beacons.
- **Beacon discipline:** ships follow signal buoys like mountain ropes in fog. Deviate meters off-line and you can emerge years away or never.
- **Jump vortices kill.** Anything organic in a forming vortex is shredded; formations approach gates in sequence, not mass.
- **Combat in hyperspace** is blind brawling in fog, sensors read beacons only, weapons fire risks breaching into realspace at random. Doctrine: fight in realspace, hide in hyperspace.

**[W]** Hyperspace is this volume's ocean: storms, sea monsters (§8.4), and drownings included. Its rules exist so that being lost means something.

### 4.4 Elder-Race Vessels

#### Shadow Battle Crab

**In brief:** A living warship, black multi-jointed spider of semi-organic armor that thinks, heals, and screams on sensors when destroyed. Its main beam slices any younger-race capital in one pass; its fighters are piloted by beings merged into the hull.

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | ~2,000 m variable (organic) **[E]** |
| Complement | Merged crew + sentient core |

## PART FIVE: WEAPONS AND EQUIPMENT

### 5.1 Personal Weapons

**In brief:** The galaxy's infantry fights with pulse plasma, Earth's PPG family is the standard by which everyone else's sidearm is measured.

- **PPG (Phased Plasma Gun) pistol:** standard sidearm; cauterizing burns, low recoil, ammo cells.
- **PPG rifle:** squad support variant; longer cell life, underbarrel options **[E]**.
- **Centauri/Narn equivalents:** energy pistols of comparable effect; Narn models favor ruggedness over elegance.
- **Minbari fighting pike:** collapsible staff weapon, a reminder that the galaxy's best soldiers still train to close distance.
- **Projectile weapons:** rare but decisive against targets immune to energy bleed; Earth marines keep slug throwers for exactly those guests.

### 5.2 Ship and Station Weapons

| Class | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Interceptor grid | Point-defense energy web | EA signature; strips incoming fire at range |
| Pulse cannons | Bolt-projectors | EA/League standard; rate-of-fire doctrine |
| Plasma streams / fusion cannons | Minbari medium guns | Secondaries on Sharlin/White Star |
| Molecular beam cannons | Sustained cutting beams | Minbari primaries; carve hulls in salvos |
| Battle lasers | Centauri heavy beams | Primus forward battery; G'Quan copies |
| Energy mines | Narn area-denial bursts | Unique tactical niche; formation-breakers |
| Slicing beam | Shadow primary | One-pass capital kill (§6.0) |
| Gravitic/neutron beams | Elder-race primaries | Vorlon scale; do not duel these |

### 5.3 Strategic Materials and Systems

- **Hull alloys:** EA morbidium steel **[E]** vs. Minbari crystalline-molecular composites vs. Shadow organic armor, three design philosophies: mass, precision, and adaptation.
- **Gravitic systems:** Minbari/Vorlon mastery versus rotating sections, the galaxy's starkest technology gap.
- **Telepathy infrastructure:** Psi Corps training, drugs (sleepers), and implant tech, telepaths are strategic materiel in every sense (§6.2).

## PART SIX: THREAT DOSSIERS

### 6.0 The Shadows

**In brief:** The oldest of the two surviving First Ones: a species ten million years old whose entire civilization runs on one theorem, *younger races evolve fastest through chaos, war, and fear.* They seed themselves among younger worlds as advisors and agents, engineer conflicts as curricula, and sleep for millennia between lessons.

**More:**

- **Philosophy as weapon:** the Shadows rarely conquer; they *teach*. Their question to every young race is "what do you want?", asked in ways that burn continents.
- **Ships:** living battle crabs (§4.4) that merge pilots into hulls; their death-scream on telepathic frequencies is a battlefield hazard all its own.
- **Servants:** corrupted humans (Morden's line), Drakh retainers **[E]**, and kept creatures, the Shadows recruit ambition, not ideology.
- **Weakness:** their method requires their own restraint, they fight to teach, not exterminate. Telepathic disruption disorients their merged crews (the last War's lesson). And their philosophy can be *answered* rather than defeated: ask the counter-question.

**Engagement rules:** Never negotiate from fear; it reads as an answer. Never trust an advisor who arrives after a disaster offering help. Always ask what lesson the attack was designed to teach.

### 6.1 The Vorlons

**In brief:** The other shepherd: order incarnate, inscrutable by policy, generous by invoice. Vorlon contact suits hide their true forms, each viewer sees something holy, because unshielded Vorlon presence rewrites younger minds.

- **Method:** long cultivation of chosen races, cryptic aid ("understanding is a three-edged sword"), and escalation so measured it reads as prophecy.
- **Threat profile:** none declared, all implied. A Vorlon cruiser absorbing a League fleet's full fire and asking "are you finished?" is diplomacy.
- **The invoice:** every Vorlon gift advances their order-versus-chaos argument. By the edition pin, both shepherds have stopped teaching and started proving, which makes them the war, not the referees.

**[W]** Write Vorlons as weather with opinions. The moment they explain themselves plainly, they shrink.
### 6.2 The Psi Corps

**In brief:** Earth's telepath institution, orphanage, school, employer, and surveillance state for the one-in-a-thousand humans born with telepathy. Officially a professional guild; functionally a state within the state that answers to itself.

- **The rating system:** P1–P12 scales ability; P12s can read and rewrite minds. Deep-scan evidence is legally admissible in Alliance courts, which makes every commercial transaction a potential mind-crime scene.
- **Institutional structure:** a Directorate of Metasensory Affairs (Earth liaison), internal Psi Cops (enforcement), and the breeding program pairing high-rated telepaths by genetic mandate, consent optional **[W]**.
- **Sleeper processing:** personality-overwrite implants ("sleepers") turn captured rogues into return-to-source assets; discovery of an implant is a station-wide security event.
- **Rogue problem:** unregistered telepaths fleeing mandatory Corps service form the station's permanent underground, Downbelow's most valuable residents and its most dangerous.
- **War relevance:** telepaths are among the few proven weapons against Shadow merged crews (§6.0), which converts an Earth civil-rights question into a strategic asset overnight.

**[W]** The Corps exists to make civil-liberties scenes carry body counts. Use them for stories where the safe choice and the right choice have different casualty lists.

### 6.3 Nightwatch and the Homegrown Threat

Earth's Ministry of Peace volunteer auxiliary: patriotic citizens deputized into political enforcement. On Babylon 5 they operated as a loyalty cell until the secession purge. Their dossier is short and necessary: fascism arrives as paperwork, wears your uniform, and quotes your own charter back at you.

### 6.4 Raiders

Pirate bands preying on the Rim's thin shipping using salvaged warships and gate ambush tactics. Professional hazard, not strategic threat, except when someone pays them to be one. Raider activity spiking around a quiet world is intelligence, not crime statistics.

### 6.5 The Drakh **[E]**

Shadow retainers and heirs: a servitor race whose fleets and cruelty outlive their patrons' withdrawal. Post-pin antagonists by design; sightings during the War were deniable and deniable things are this manual's favorite kind.

## PART SEVEN: COMMAND AND ORGANIZATION

### 7.0 Chain of Command

```
EARTH ALLIANCE SENATE (pre-secession)   — charter authority
        |
Babylon Project oversight / Advisory Council
        |
COMMANDER, BABYLON 5 STATION            — civil + military authority
        |
   +--------+-------------+---------------+
   |        |             |               |
SECURITY  OPERATIONS   MEDICAL         DIPLOMATIC STAFF
(station   (C&C,        (Medlab 1-3,     (one delegation
 police,    fighters,    quarantine)       per power)
 customs)   docking)
   |
NIGHTWATCH (purged at pin date)  — loyalty cell, dissolved by force
```

**In brief:** The commander's authority is doubled (civil charter + military garrison) and therefore permanently conflicted; the Advisory Council gives four embassies plus League voices formal say in station governance, a parliament disguised as a staff meeting.

- **Medical override:** Medlab holds quarantine authority over all traffic, including delegations.
- **Customs as foreign policy:** every inspection is a message to somebody's government.
- **Post-secession reality:** Earth orders are void aboard; the council recognizes the station as sovereign by necessity. Every diplomatic consequence of that recognition is story fuel.

### 7.1 Personnel Policies

- **Telepath registration:** Earth law requires Corps membership or suppression drugs; B5 honors rogue sanctuary informally, a standing treaty violation with Earth, maintained deliberately.
- **Garrison composition:** Earthforce Marines and fighter wing; post-pin, augmented by Minbari-religious-caste volunteers and League detachments **[W]**.
- **Rules of engagement:** charter-first, weapons only after identification and warning; delegations are inviolate short of overt attack.

## PART EIGHT: A GALAXY BESTIARY

*Phenomena, artifacts, and hazards.*

### 8.0 The Great Machine (Epsilon III)

**What:** A planet-spanning machine built half a million years ago, still running beneath Epsilon III, sentient-adjacent, capable of time vision and dimensional manipulation, guarded by a lone custodian who cannot leave.
**Where:** The station's own sky.
**Cost of contact:** It chooses operators, not the reverse. It has shown the future; the future took notes.

### 8.2 Babylon 4

**What:** The previous Babylon station, vanished hours after going operational in 2253(?) **[W]**, later confirmed extracted through time six years *backward*, crewed by figures from the last Shadow War. The station that fought a war a millennium ago and is now the Minbari religion's flagship legend.
**Where:** The far past, fully staffed.
**Cost of contact:** Time travel here is a one-way conscription: those who go stay. Any sighting of B4 is either archaeology or prophecy, and no instrument distinguishes them.

### 8.3 Soul Hunters

**What:** An order that captures the souls of the dying at the moment of death, storing them in crystal spheres, believing they preserve greatness, blind to the imprisonment they inflict.
**Where:** Deep-space vessels, solitary.
**Cost of contact:** Proof of afterlife used as a filing system. Their spheres contain witnesses to everything.

### 8.4 Hyperspace Phenomena

**What:** The middle dimension's native hazards, gravitational wakes that shred beacons, "things that live in hyperspace" reported since the first gates, and the static between layers where lost ships drift forever.
**Where:** Everywhere ships travel without realspace reference.
**Cost of contact:** Navigation discipline is life. Every beacon chain has a graveyard at its edge.

### 8.5 The Drafa Plague

**What:** A Markab-origin plague, 100% fatal and transmitted by faith-linked behavior patterns; it erased a species in months before Medlab isolation stopped it aboard.
**Where:** Quarantine archives; any world that hid its dead.
**Cost of contact:** Certainty. The plague killed through the behaviors belief encouraged, epidemiology as theology lesson.

### 8.6 First One Artifacts

**What:** Relics of the departed elder races: jumpgates themselves, the Great Machine class installations, gate-openers that reach places with no names.
**Where:** The Rim, mostly.
**Cost of contact:** Each artifact is a phone line to something that left. Answering is optional; ringing back is not.

### 8.7 Techno-Mages **[E]**

**What:** An order of technologists whose "magic" is sufficiently advanced technology, nanotech familiars, energy cloaks, force blasts, descended from a First One teaching lineage. They withdrew before the Shadow War began, on the principle that their patrons' patrons were about to become obvious.
**Where:** Scattered; gathered once, secretly, when the War started.
**Cost of contact:** They know what's coming and decline to say. Their rule, *do not reveal the technology*, makes them unreliable allies and unforgettable encounters.

### 8.8 Vorlon Encounter Hazard

**What:** Unshielded proximity to an un-suited Vorlon rewrites younger-race perception: each viewer sees a being of their own faith. Glandular contact (poison) enforces the secret.
**Where:** Anywhere Ambassador Kosh walks.
**Cost of contact:** Revelation at the speed of faith. Two people describing the same Vorlon will fight before they compare notes.
