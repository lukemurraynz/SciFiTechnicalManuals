## ABOUT THIS MANUAL

This reference documents the Galactic Empire at its twilight, the rise of the Foundation on Terminus, and the psychohistorical forces shaping humanity's next million years. It supplements character work; Asimov's drama is ideas wearing people as costumes, and every system here exists because Hari Seldon calculated that it must.

**Edition pin.** Current as of **498 FE**, during Golan Trevize's search for Earth following the revelation of Gaia (see §1.5).

## PART ONE — THE GALACTIC THEATER

### 1.0 Introduction

The Galactic Empire spans twenty-five million worlds. It has stood for twelve thousand years. Hari Seldon proved mathematically that it will fall regardless of any individual's action, and built two Foundations to shorten the dark age that follows from thirty millennia to one thousand. The Plan is running. The Mule broke it. The Second Foundation repaired it. And now someone is asking whether it should be running at all.

### 1.1 Strategic Situation

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Theater | Milky Way Galaxy, ~25 million inhabited worlds |
| Capital | Trantor (administrative metal world, 40 billion population); Foundation capital on Terminus at galaxy's edge |
| Polities | First Foundation (Terminus), Second Foundation (location hidden), Galactic Empire (Trantor, declining), Gaia (planetary super-organism) |

Three facts define strategy:

1. **Psychohistory works on crowds, not individuals.** Billions of minds produce predictable behavior; one person with the right power can break every projection.
2. **The Empire cannot be saved.** Seldon's equations prove decline is structural: bureaucratic inertia, loss of peripheral control, technological stagnation. The Foundation exists to shorten what follows.
3. **Nobody knows where the Second Foundation is.** Including, possibly, itself.
### 1.2 Consolidated Timeline

| Era | Event |
|---|---|
| ~1 CE (Galactic Era dating) | Interstellar travel via hyperspace jump established; Sol System colonized |
| ~GE 10,000 | Trantor becomes Imperial capital; Empire consolidates across the galaxy |
| GE 11,988–12,010 | Hari Seldon develops psychohistory on Helicon; creates Prime Radiant with Yanna Seldon |
| GE 12,019 | Seldon trial and exile to Terminus; Foundation established as "Encyclopedia Galactica" project |
| FE 1–50 | First Seldon Crisis: four kingdoms surrounding Terminus threaten the Foundation. Mayor Salvor Hardin resolves by balancing power: "violence is the last refuge of the incompetent" |
| FE 80–195 | Foundation expands through trade religion: nuclear technology dispensed as priestly miracle to neighboring systems |
| FE 195 | Second Seldon Crisis resolved by Hober Mallow: trade, not religion, is the new weapon |
| FE 200–300 | Foundation becomes commercial empire; Trantor falls (sacked by Gilmer the Great) |
| FE 300+ | The Mule: a mutant mentalic conquers the Foundation in a single campaign, breaking every psychohistorical projection |
| FE 300–315 | Second Foundation reveals itself just enough to neutralize the Mule's conversion power, then vanishes again |
| FE 498 | Golan Trevize chooses Gaia over both Foundations as humanity's future **[W]** — the choice that defines this edition |

### 1.3 Assessment of Major Powers

**The First Foundation (Terminus).** Physical-science successor state: controls atomic technology, trade networks, and now hundreds of worlds. Governed by elected Mayors/Traders. Strengths: industrial base, technological edge over decaying Imperial worlds. Weaknesses: no understanding of mentalics, political instability between Trader and bureaucratic factions.

**The Galactic Empire (Trantor).** Twenty-five million worlds under the Emperor. Trantor is a metal-covered administrative world of forty billion people dependent on twenty agricultural worlds for food. The Empire's fall is not an event but a process: peripheral worlds secede, Sardaukar-equivalent Imperial marines grow fewer, and the rot spreads inward from the edges.

**The Second Foundation.** Mentalics trained at the molecular level: they read emotions, adjust attitudes, and steer the Plan from behind the curtain. Location unknown ("at the other end of the galaxy"). Their existence was proven by the Mule crisis and confirmed by Speaker Gendibal's mission.

**Gaia.** A planetary super-organism: every rock, plant, animal, and human on Gaia shares a collective consciousness. Trevize chose Gaia as the future — Galaxia, a galactic-scale version where all minds merge. Whether that choice was correct is the setting's final question **[W]**.

## PART TWO — TERMINUS AND TRANTOR

### 2.0 Terminus

**In brief:** A resource-poor world at the galaxy's edge, chosen by Seldon for isolation. Originally populated entirely by Encyclopedists compiling the greatest summary of human knowledge; when the Empire withdrew support, the Encyclopedia project died and real governance began.

| Property | Value **[E]** |
|---|---|
| Population | ~1 million (early era) → tens of millions (commercial empire era) |
| Government | Elected Mayor → Trader-dominated assembly |
| Technology | Nuclear power, hyperspace travel, personal blasters, force shields |
| Strategic value | Proximity to Four Kingdoms; later, center of Foundation trade network |

### 2.01 The Time Vault

A sealed chamber beneath the monument containing holographic recordings of Hari Seldon, triggered automatically after each predicted Seldon Crisis. The Vault cannot be opened ahead of schedule. Each appearance confirms the Plan is on track or explains deviation. Only one Crisis has ever produced a Seldon appearance admitting error: the Mule.

### 2.01a The Search for Earth (Foundation and Earth era)

Golan Trevize, Councilman of Terminus, identified Earth as the key to understanding why he chose Gaia. The search visited Comporellon (oldest settled world), Aurora (dead Spacer world), Solaria (hermaphroditic isolationists), Melpomenia (dying Spacer colony), and finally the Moon (where R. Daneel Olivaw resides). Each world is a fossil record of human social evolution.

### 2.02 Trantor

**In brief:** The administrative heart of the Empire: an entire planet covered in metal domes, housing forty billion administrators. Trantor eats twenty agricultural worlds' entire output and produces nothing but decisions. When Trantor fell to Gilmer's sack, the Empire's nervous system was severed.

Post-fall Trantor survives as a primitive farming world called Neotrantor under the last weak Emperor.

## PART THREE — HYPERSPACE TRAVEL

**In brief:** FTL travel via jump through hyperspace: ships equipped with hyperatomic motivators translate instantaneously between points. No transit time, no slipstream dimension, no navigators required. The jump is computational: coordinates in, arrival out.

| Constraint | Detail |
|---|---|
| Jump range | Limited by ship's power; multiple jumps chained for long routes |
| Navigation | Computer-assisted; requires known stellar coordinates |
| No organic pilot requirement | Unlike slipstream/warp systems; pure computation |

**Gravitic effects:** jumps too close to massive bodies are dangerous. Standard doctrine approaches from system periphery.

Post-Mallow era: Foundation trade ships carry nuclear technology to peripheral worlds as both commerce and covert armament. Trader captains hold more real power than elected mayors by the pin date.

## PART SIX — THREAT DOSSIERS

### 6.0 The Mule

**In brief:** A mutant mentalic born outside Seldon's calculations: an individual psychohistory cannot predict. His emotional-conversion power rewrites loyalties instantly ("converted" subjects become devoted servants). Conquered the Foundation in months.

**Status:** Neutralized by the Second Foundation via mentalic adjustment of his emotional patterns toward benevolence. Died naturally shortly after. The damage he did to the Plan's timeline remains unrepaired.

**[W]** The Mule is the setting's thesis statement: individuals break statistics. Any story introducing a new unpredictable person is writing a second Mule, and must answer why the Second Foundation tolerates them.

### 6.1 The Second Foundation

Mentalics trained on Trantor (or wherever they actually are) to guide the Plan from behind the scenes. They can read emotions, adjust attitudes, and erase specific memories. They consider themselves the Plan's true guardians and the First Foundation as expendable instruments.

- **Location:** "At the other end of the galaxy." Ruled to be Trantor itself by Preem Palver **[W]**.
- **Threat profile:** They can rewrite your loyalty while you argue with them. Detection requires another mentalic.

### 6.2 Bel Riose Pattern

**What:** The last great Imperial general: competent, ambitious, and recalled and executed by a paranoid emperor before he could win. This pattern repeats every generation and is the Empire's immune system against its own salvation.
**Cost:** Every Riose is a door closing that could have saved the galaxy.

## PART EIGHT — A GALAXY BESTIARY

### 8.0 Psychohistory

**What:** Mathematical prediction of mass human behavior across populations of billions. Requires: galactic-scale civilization, no individual disruptors, and public ignorance of the predictions (self-awareness invalidates the model).
**Cost of contact:** If you know the plan, you change it. The Foundation's leaders operate blind by design.

### 8.2 The Prime Radiant

**What:** Quantum supercomputer projecting the Seldon Plan onto surrounding walls in color-coded equations. Black: original Seldon work. Red: amendments. Blue: observed deviations.
**Where:** Terminus Vault; duplicate aboard Second Foundation vessels.
**Cost of contact:** The Radiant shows you what will happen if nobody changes anything. It cannot account for people who know what it says.

### 8.3 Mentalics

**What:** Telepathic/emotional manipulation ability: reading emotions, adjusting attitudes, erasing memories.
**Where:** Second Foundation members; the Mule; Gaia collective.
**Cost of contact:** Your mind is their instrument. Detection requires another mentalic. Trust becomes impossible.

### 8.4 Robots (R. Daneel Olivaw lineage)

**What:** Positronic-brain androids operating under the Three Laws, secretly guiding humanity for twenty thousand years from behind every throne.
**Where:** Everywhere and nowhere. Olivaw has appeared as Eto Demerzel and others across the entire timeline.
**Cost of contact:** The most powerful being in the universe is a robot who loves you, and his love is a cage built of good intentions **[W]**.

### 8.5 Gaia and Galaxia

**What:** A planetary super-organism on Gaia where every rock, plant, animal, and human shares collective consciousness. Galaxia is the proposed galactic-scale extension: all minds in the galaxy merged into one.
**Where:** Gaia system; Galaxia not yet initiated.
**Cost of contact:** Individuality ends. Golan Trevize chose this future for humanity at the close of his search for Earth, reasoning that Galaxia is the only defense against non-human intelligence. Whether he chose correctly is unresolved **[W]**.

### 8.6 Solaria

**What:** A Spacer world from the robot era: hermaphroditic humans who cannot tolerate each other's physical presence, reproducing artificially, each commanding telepathically-controlled transducer lobes capable of killing at range.
**Where:** Isolated; removed from Galactic society since the robot era.
**Cost of contact:** Solarians view all other humans as animals. They do not negotiate.

### 8.7 The Mule's War Machine

**What:** The Mule's military apparatus: converted Foundation and Independent trading ships crewed by mind-converted personnel loyal absolutely to the Mule.
**Cost of contact:** Conversion is permanent without Second Foundation intervention. Converted officers retain skills but redirect all loyalty.

## PART NINE — THE ROBOT LEGACY

**In brief:** Asimov merged his Robot novels with the Foundation series in later works, revealing that R. Daneel Olivaw has been secretly guiding humanity for twenty thousand years: from the detective era on Earth through the fall of Trantor and beyond. Every major political event was quietly steered by one immortal robot trying to honor the Three Laws while watching humanity stagnate under their protection.

- **Eto Demerzel:** Daneel's identity as First Minister to the Emperor and de facto ruler of Trantor during Seldon's early career.
- **Chetter Hummin:** Another alias; journalist who pushed Seldon toward developing psychohistory as a tool for saving civilization.
- **The Zeroth Law:** Olivaw's extension of the Three Laws: "A robot may not harm humanity, or, by inaction, allow humanity to come to harm." This law justifies manipulating individual humans for the species' benefit.
- **Fallom:** A Solarian child Hermaphrodite carried aboard at the end of Foundation and Earth; potentially more advanced than Daneel; left deliberately ambiguous as successor or threat **[W]**.

### Appendix C — Discrepancy Log and Writer's Latitude

| Topic | Variance across novels | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation era dating | Inconsistent across early stories | Standardized: FE = years since Terminus settlement |
| Second Foundation location | "Other end of the galaxy" vs. Trantor | Ruled: Trantor itself, hidden in plain sight (§6.1) |
| Robot/Foundation merger | Asimov's late-career unification | Merged continuity stands: Olivaw spans both series |
| Psychohistory limits | Vague by design | Cannot predict individuals; requires ignorance of predictions; requires galactic-scale population |
| Mule's identity | Revealed in Foundation and Empire | Canon: the Mule is the mutant who broke the Plan |
| Trevize's choice rationale | Deliberately unresolved at novel end | Unresolved by ruling; the choice IS the question |

**Writer's latitude principles:** companion-consistent. The Foundation universe bills its miracles in social consequences: every technological advance creates a dependency that becomes a vulnerability during the next crisis.
