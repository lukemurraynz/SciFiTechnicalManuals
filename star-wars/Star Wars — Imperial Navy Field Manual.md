## PART ONE: THE GALACTIC THEATER

### 1.0 Introduction

The Clone Wars proved that democracy cannot govern a galaxy. The Empire proved that it need not. The Navy is the argument's final punctuation: a million worlds held by fear, order, and hull tonnage.

### 1.1 Strategic Situation

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Theater | Galactic — Core to Outer Rim |
| Fleet | 25,000+ Imperial-class Star Destroyers **[E]**; sector groups beyond count |
| Insurgency | "Rebel Alliance" — terrorist cells, stolen fighters, one defecting Mon Calamari fleet |
| Superweapon | DS-1 battle station operational; doctrine of fear (Tarkin) in effect |

Defining facts:

1. **Fear outperforms fleets per credit.** The Tarkin Doctrine: rule through the threat of force, demonstrated rarely and catastrophically.
2. **The Rim is terrain, not territory.** A million worlds cannot each be garrisoned; sector groups hold chokepoints and let silence govern.
3. **The Jedi are ended.** Their remnant is folklore with a lightsaber.
### 1.2 Consolidated Timeline

| Era | Event |
|---|---|
| ~25,000 years ago | Hyperdrive unification; the Old Republic and Jedi Order found |
| ~1,000 years ago | Ruusan Reformations; Republic militia era begins |
| 22–19 BBY | Clone Wars: separatist crisis resolved by Chancellor Palpatine's ascension; Jedi purged; Empire declared |
| 19–5 BBY | Consolidation: Senate retained, power migrated to Moffs; rebel cells seed |
| 5–1 BBY | Open insurgency: stolen plans, convoy raids, Mon Calamari defection brewing |
| 0 BBY | Death Star operational. **Edition pin** |

### 1.3 Powers Assessment (Summary)

**The Empire:** unlimited industry, limited imagination, fleet supremacy bought with standardization that rebels reverse-engineer faster than doctrine updates. **The Rebellion:** no industry, total initiative, every carrier of a blaster is a vote the Senate lost. **Neutral powers:** Hutt cartels, corporate guilds, and Core financiers profiting from both.

### 1.4 Founding Technologies

**In brief:** Four systems underpin galactic order: hyperdrive classes (0.5 Imperial standard) binding a million worlds to one capital's clockwork; blaster energy weapons (logistics as cartridge cylinders); droid labor/computation (memory-wiped on schedule for loyalty); and the battle station, superlaser deterrence made literal.

### 1.5 Status Quo Snapshot

| Domain | Status |
|---|---|
| Senate | Dissolved; regional governors hold direct power |
| Fleet posture | Sector-group dispersal; Death Star demonstrates superweapon deterrence |
| Rebellion | Disorganized cells; Mon Calamari defection pending **[W]** |
| Force users | Hunted by the Inquisitorius; two remain of note to the Emperor |

**[W]** Every snapshot in an Imperial manual is also a censorship decision. What is omitted is doctrine.

## PART TWO: THE DEATH STAR (STATION DOSSIER)

### 2.0 Overview

**In brief:** A 120 km battle station carrying a hypermatter-reactor-fed superlaser capable of destroying planets, the Empire's ultimate expression of the Tarkin Doctrine.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 120 km |
| Complement | ~1.7 million military + 400,000 droids **[E]** |
| Superlaser | Planet-cracking single shot (Alderaan verified); recharge cycle measured in hours **[W]** — treat as day-scale for planning |
| Defense | Turbolaser grids rated against fleets; fighter complement ~7,000 TIEs **[E]** |
| Flaw | Exhaust port vulnerability — *corrected in revised schematics* **[W]** |

**Doctrine:** the station exists so it never fires twice at the same audience. Its weakness was procedural, not structural: arrogance is a targeting solution.

### 2.01 Station Internals (officer orientation)

| Zone | Function |
|---|---|
| Superlaser control | Firing chain: reactor → capacitors → focusing lens array — eight-dish convergence |
| Command sector | Grand Moff's operations center; conference citadel |
| Detention level (Level 5) | Holding cells, blast doors, the trash compactor maintenance levels below **[W]** |
| Hangar bays | TIE wings, troop transports, shuttle berths |
| Tractor beam control | Primary capture system — boarding doctrine anchor |
| Reactor core | Hypermatter annihilation chamber; unshielded access is the station's one honest weakness **[W]** |

**Garrison doctrine:** stormtrooper legions handle boarders and prisoners; naval troops man the guns. Escape-pod protocols exist but are discouraged, morale through inevitability.

## PART THREE: HYPERSPACE OPERATIONS

**In brief:** Hyperdrive classes (0.5 Imperial standard → slower) multiply realspace travel times; navicomputers plot multi-jump routes along established lanes.

- **Lanes are infrastructure:** Hydian Way, Perlemian, Corellian Run, commerce and fleet movement share roads.
- **Interdiction:** Interdictor cruisers project gravity wells that drag ships from hyperspace, blockade and ambush doctrine.
- **Rebel hit-and-fade doctrine:** small strikes on convoys, then jump. Counter-doctrine: convoy density, patrol screens, and accepting attrition as loyalty filtering **[W]**.

## PART SIX: THREAT DOSSIERS **[W: read as doctrine, verify before relying]**

### 6.0 The Rebel Alliance

Terrorist coalition of failed senators, criminal smugglers, and defector pilots operating from hidden bases. Strengths: small-unit initiative, stolen technology, public sympathy among Rim worlds weakened by "order." Weaknesses: no manufacturing base, dependence on secrecy, hence the Empire's informant economy and base-hunting operations.

#### Alliance Cell Structure (intelligence assessment) **[W]**

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| High Command (council) | Fleet decisions; location changes with every leak |
| Fighter squadrons | Red/Gold/Blue group designations; veteran cadre |
| Sector cells | Partisan networks on occupied worlds |
| Mon Cal conversion yards | Capital-ship manufacturing from civilian liners — the rebellion's shipyards |
| Bothan spynet **[E]** | Intelligence network; every major Imperial secret they've delivered has cost them dearly |

**Assessment caveat:** official figures understate. Every ISD captain who has dismissed "farmers with stolen fighters" as a threat has contributed to the Rebellion's veteran pool.

### 6.2 The Force

An energy field formerly mediated by the Jedi Order; now an Inquisitorius hunting ground. Force-sensitive individuals are strategic assets or threats depending on loyalty screening. The Emperor's interest in Force matters is above Navy pay grade.

### 6.3 Crime Syndicates

Hutt-space cartels: tolerated so long as tribute flows and rebellion doesn't route through their lanes.

## PART EIGHT: IMPERIAL BESTIARY

### 8.0 The Force

**What:** The binding field of the galaxy; sensibles number one in millions.
**Cost of contact:** Discipline or death. The Emperor prefers recruitment.

### 8.2 Uncharted Regions

**What:** The galactic west, unmapped, uncolonized, expensive to survey.
**Cost of contact:** Unknown. The Navy has lost probe droids there and reports none.

### 8.3 Hutt Space

**What:** Semi-autonomous cartel territory the Empire taxes rather than governs.
**Cost of contact:** Diplomacy conducted in bribes and hostages.

### 8.4 Kessel Run / Smuggling Corridors

**What:** Illegal shipping routes threading mines and anomalies; measured in parsecs of shortcut bravado.
**Cost of contact:** Contraband, credits, and occasionally princesses.
