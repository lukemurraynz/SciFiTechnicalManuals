## ABOUT THIS MANUAL

This manual documents the Type 40 time and relative dimension in space capsule: a living time machine older than most civilizations, currently masquerading as a 1963 Metropolitan police telephone box somewhere in the universe.

**Edition pin.** This manual covers the capsule in its **post-Time War, current-regeneration era**, owner: the Doctor. Ownership disputes are settled by the fact that she stole him first **[W]**.

## PART ONE — DIMENSIONAL TRANSCENDENTALISM

### 1.0 Introduction

The TARDIS is bigger on the inside. Not "appears bigger": the interior exists in a separate dimensional plane tethered to the exterior shell. Interior volume has been mapped at effectively infinite; occupants have gotten lost for decades within its cloisters and swimming pools (the pool is near the wardrobe; this is not an excuse for either).

**In brief:** The shell is a door. Everything else is another universe with a doorbell.

### 1.1 Core Systems

| System | Function | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Chameleon Circuit | Shell disguises per environment | **Broken since 1963**: stuck as police box. Every repair attempt produces a police box |
| Time Rotor | Central column oscillating during flight | Currently glass panelling; groans audibly in flight (the sound IS the system) |
| Heart of the TARDIS | Living sentience powering all functions | Sentient. Opinionated. Chose the Doctor as much as he chose her **[W]** |
| Translation Field | Telepathic language matrix for crew/companions | Always on; makes every alien tongue English |
| Perception Filter | Makes the box "unnoticed" in plain sight | Functional when the plot allows it **[W]** |
| HADS (Hostile Action Displacement System) | Auto-dematerializes under attack | Frequently disabled by owners who prefer drama |
| Cloister Bell | Warning signal for imminent catastrophe | Sounds only when the universe itself is threatened |
| Temporal Grace | No physical violence possible inside the capsule while operational | Time Lord engineering; fails when the TARDIS is dying |
| Eye of Harmony | Exploded black hole (star) providing infinite power | Located beneath the console room; opening without authorization is a capital crime on Gallifrey |
| Zero Room | Dampens all sensory input for recovery from psychic trauma | Exists "inside" the TARDIS interior; accessible only when needed |
| Swimming Pool / Library / Wardrobe | Interior rooms exceeding exterior shell volume by orders of magnitude | Pool is near the wardrobe; this is not an excuse for either |
| Chameleon Arch | Biologically rewrites the owner into a human; stores Time Lord essence in a fob watch | Emergency identity concealment; memories return when watch is opened |
| Translation Matrix | Auto-translates all written/spoken languages for crew and companions | Linked to the TARDIS telepathic field, not the companion's own ability |

### 1.2 Flight Doctrine

Dematerialization requires the Time Rotor engaged and coordinates set on the six-sided console. The console room has cycled through several designs (white gleaming chrome to gothic coral to steampunk glass); the ship redecorates according to her moods between regenerations.

- **Flight is normally silent-instantaneous** from outside perspective.
- **The groaning wheeze** of materialization is a fault nobody will fix because everyone recognizes it.
- **Doors open on impact tolerance:** the wooden door has survived bullets, Dalek fire, and the vacuum of space. It is not just a door **[W]**.

## PART TWO — TIME TRAVEL DOCTRINE

### 3.1 The Laws of Time (as practiced)

1. **Non-intervention in fixed events.** Fixed points in time are structural to history. Attempting to alter them causes catastrophe or is itself the event.
2. **No crossing your own timeline** without Blinovitch Limitation precautions: contact between a person's younger/older selves triggers energy discharge and worse.
3. **Paradoxes attract Reapers**: temporal entities that consume paradox sources, erasing the area from history.
4. **The Time War changed everything.** Pre-War Time Lord law assumed an institution enforcing it. Post-War, the last Time Lord enforces it by mood.

### 3.2 Temporal Phenomena

| Phenomenon | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Blinovitch Limitation Effect** | Energy discharge on self-timeline contact |
| **Time fissures/cracks** | Windows across time; things fall through both ways |
| **Fixed points** | Events history refuses to change (Rome burns; Capone falls) |
| **Reapers** | Paradox predators: erase contaminated zones from causality |
| **Bootstrap loops** | Information with no origin; stable but philosophically radioactive |

## PART THREE — THREAT DOSSIERS

### 3.0 Daleks

**In brief:** Mutant Kaled cores in armored travel machines: engineered hatred with tank treads and a plunger. Created by Davros on Skaro; survived their own creators' war repeatedly.

- **Doctrine:** EXTERMINATE as foreign policy. Time travel capability acquired during the Time War.
- **Weakness:** suction cup arm not rated for stairs (historically); belief systems immune to reason entirely.

### 3.1 Cybermen

**In brief:** Parallel-evolution humanity: Mondasian and Cybus variants converting organics into emotionless metal soldiers via "upgrading."

**Engagement rules:** Gold dust/nitro-9 disrupt respiratory systems; emotional suppressors fail when feelings leak back through. Cybermen win by patience and numbers.

### 3.2 The Master

**In brief:** The Doctor's oldest friend and mirror: another Time Lord, renegade, resurrected repeatedly, sometimes end-of-universe ambitious.

| Regeneration era | Modus operandi |
|---|---|
| Early incarnations | Diplomatic infiltration; UNIT sabotage; Auton alliance |
| Traken revival | Stole the Matrix; attempted Dalek alliance |
| End of Time | Immortality Gate; Master Race (every human converted to Master copies) |
| Missy | Cyberman army from dead humans; Nethersphere afterlife database; Time Lord Matrix infiltration |

**Status:** Always alive somewhere. That is the point of him **[W]**.

### 3.3 Weeping Angels

**In brief:** Quantum-locked predators: they exist only while unobserved, moving faster than sight when unseen. Images of an angel become angels. The only defense: don't blink.

### 3.4 The Time War

**What:** The Dalek-Time Lord conflict that burned the universe's history books. Billions of years of warfare compressed into one terrible day by temporal weapons. Ended when the Doctor used the Moment to destroy both sides simultaneously (Dalek fleet and Time Lord civilization together at the close of the last day of the War). Gallifrey was later revealed saved but displaced into a pocket universe.

**Cost of contact:** Survivors' guilt with a galaxy attached. The Doctor's refusal to use his name after the War is a wound, not a fashion choice.

## PART FOUR - TIME LORD PHYSIOLOGY AND SOCIETY

### 5.0 Biology

Two hearts (backup circulatory system), respiratory bypass (survive strangulation and vacuum exposure briefly), regeneration on terminal death (cellular rewrite into new body/personality; memories intact; personality shifts within core identity), telepathic contact with other Time Lords and their TARDIS, body temperature runs cold (60°F / 15°C baseline).

### 5.1 Gallifreyan Institutions

| Institution | Function |

| High Council | Cardinal-level governance |

| The Matrix | Supercomputer containing extracted minds of dead Time Lords; predictive and archival; can be entered physically via headplug |
| Academies | Prydonius (politics/the Doctor), Arcalia (science), Patrex (art) |

## PART FIVE - THE DOCTOR

**In brief:** Last (mostly) of the Time Lords, thief of a faulty TARDIS, wanderer since the Time War ended his people. Regenerates instead of dying: twelve-plus faces, one continuous memory, increasingly poor at goodbyes.

- **Companions:** the TARDIS travels best with humans aboard; they keep him kind.
- **The sonic screwdriver:** utility tool, magic wand by reputation; does not open deadbolts (wood). It is a confidence trick with a power supply, and it works.
- **Rules:** the Doctor lies about following rules, then follows them anyway at terrible cost.

**[W]** The Doctor is the format's exception that proves every rule in this set: a technical manual about a character who defeats manuals.

## PART SEVEN - BESTIARY OF TIME AND SPACE

### 7.0 Fixed Points

**What:** Events woven into history so tightly that changing them unravels time itself.
**Cost of contact:** Even the Doctor obeys this one. Especially the Doctor.

### 7.1 The Time War

**What:** The Dalek-Time Lord conflict that burned the universe's history books: billions of years of warfare compressed into one terrible day, ending with the Moment and a choice the Doctor has regenerated around.
**Cost of contact:** Survivors' guilt with a galaxy attached.

### 7.2 Regeneration

**What:** Time Lord death-cheating: cellular renewal into a new body/personality, memories intact. Limited cycles historically (twelve); circumstances have expanded the budget **[W]**.
**Cost of contact:** You survive. Someone else wakes up wearing your life.

### 7.3 The Silence

**What:** Religious order whose members are forgotten the instant they leave your sight. Counting marks on skin recommended.
**Where:** Behind you, historically speaking.
